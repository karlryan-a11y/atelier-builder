00

Lock the rules before anyone touches a piece

Chelsea + Karl · prerequisite

Half the mess is inconsistent tagging. Fix the rulebook first, or the sweep just creates a new mess.

  1. Define Danielle's category taxonomy with her. The full list of categories she wants, in her words. This should already have happened — if it hasn't, it happens before the sweep starts. Chelsea owns this conversation.
  2. Write the multi-tag rules. This is the piece we keep missing. An earring is tagged Earrings but never also Jewelry, so searching "jewelry" finds nothing. Decide which granular tags roll up under a parent (Earrings / Bracelets / Necklaces / Rings → also Jewelry; Jackets / Blazers / Coats → also Outerwear), and which stay standalone (e.g. 49ers). Every piece gets tagged under every category it belongs to.
  3. Karl/Claude cleans up behind the scenes first. Before the team starts, we restore the ~80 real pieces that were hidden by mistake, so they're already on Atelier when the sweep reaches them. The team never touches "hidden" — they just find pieces on Atelier and confirm them.
Prerequisite, non-negotiable

Do not start Phase 1 until the taxonomy and the multi-tag rules are written down. Everyone tags off the same rulebook, or you re-do it.

01

The folder-by-folder sweep

Stylists · in parallel by category

This is the core of the whole thing. Work one Drive folder (one category) at a time, with the Collection page open beside it — Style → Categorize → Collection, filtered to that category. Everything you need is already there: name, brand, category, multiple categories, color, internal notes.

  1. Count garments, not photos. We shot most pieces as garment + tag — two photos per item. So a folder of 194 photos is ~97 pieces. Count the garment shots; the tag shots are the reference that goes with a piece, not separate pieces.
  2. Filter the Collection to that category and read the “X of Y confirmed on Drive” counter. Use “Unconfirmed only” to see what's still left.
  3. Go garment-by-garment. Find each piece on Atelier and confirm it:
    • Open the piece (pencil) and check it's right: brand (read the tag photo), color, title/description, category, and every additional tag it belongs under (per the Phase 0 rules — e.g. an earring is also Jewelry). Fix anything wrong right here.
    • If the tag is missing or illegible → mark it Unknown brand (it goes to the Unknowns list, Phase 3), don't guess.
    • Click Confirm on Google Drive. The card turns green and the counter ticks up.
  4. If you can't find a piece, check all of Atelier before calling it missing:
Wrong category on Atelier
Search the whole collection — found under another category → recategorize it, verify, confirm. (This is most of the "outerwear looks half-empty" problem — the jackets are just filed elsewhere.)
Wrong Drive folder
It might just be misfiled in Drive (a belt in the Tops folder). You'll catch it when you sweep that other folder — don't call it missing yet.
Nowhere on Atelier at all
Not under any category → move both photos (garment + tag) into a “Not Found on Atelier” subfolder inside that Drive folder, keeping the category. Those get re-digitized in Phase 2.
Two live copies
You already confirmed this exact piece and here's a second one → it's a duplicate. Archive the weaker one (now logged with who/when/why).

A category is done when the counter reads Y of Y confirmed and every photo in its Drive folder is either confirmed on Atelier or sitting in that folder's “Not Found” subfolder. Nothing left unexplained.

Chelsea + Karl/Claude

Stylists → Chelsea → Danielle (last)

Atelier → Folder

Watch-outs — the things that quietly break this

Read before you start
1
Count garments, not photos. ~2 photos per piece (garment + tag). Counting photos will make every folder look twice as big and manufacture fake "gaps."
2
Search ALL of Atelier before "Not Found." A piece missing from the Belts view may be live under the wrong category, or its photo may be misfiled in the wrong Drive folder. "Not Found" means confirmed nowhere on Atelier — otherwise we re-digitize things that already exist and make duplicates.
3
Verify both directions. Folder→Atelier is you clicking Confirm on each piece. Atelier→Folder is the counter hitting Y of Y — every live piece confirmed. If a piece can never be confirmed against a real folder photo, it's a phantom; investigate it.
4
Taxonomy + multi-tag rules first. If they're not written down before the sweep, everyone tags differently and it gets re-done.
5
The 703 re-processed pieces. Most kept their original filename and reconcile cleanly — but any piece we downloaded, remade in ChatGPT, and re-uploaded may have a new filename. Those need a human eye; don't assume the auto-match caught them.
6
The honest limit. This guarantees everything we photographed is complete and correct. A piece Danielle owns that was never photographed can't be in the folder or the system — that's an inventory question for her, not a digitization gap. Name that boundary so "100%" means the same thing to everyone.
